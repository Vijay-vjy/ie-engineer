    Worked example

    How SAM is calculated, step by step

    1. Take readings. Five cycles of “Collar attach”: 30, 32, 31, 29, 33 s
    2. Average observed time. 155 ÷ 5 = 31 s = 0.517 min
    3. Apply rating (90%). Basic time = 0.517 × 0.90 = 0.465 min
    4. Add allowances (15%). SAM = 0.465 × 1.15 = 0.535 min
    5. Targets. 60 ÷ 0.535 = 112 pcs/hr at 100%, 78 pcs/hr at 70% efficiency.
    Answer
    SAM = 0.535 min

    Target: 112 pcs/hr at 100% · 78 pcs/hr at 70%

    Reference

    Formulas & guidance

    Basic time
    Observed time × Rating ÷ 100

    Normalises the observed time to a standard pace.

    SAM / SMV
    Basic time × (1 + Allowance ÷ 100)

    Adds personal, fatigue, delay, machine and bundle allowances.

    Target per hour
    60 ÷ SAM × Efficiency ÷ 100

    Pieces one operator should produce in an hour.

    Target output
    Operators × Minutes × Eff% ÷ SAM

    Line output for a shift at planned efficiency.

    Operators required
    Output × SAM ÷ (Minutes × Eff%)

    Manpower needed to deliver a daily target.

    Line efficiency
    Output × SAM ÷ (Operators × Minutes) × 100

    Share of available minutes turned into standard minutes.

    Time study tips

    • Time at least 5–10 cycles.
    • Study a trained operator using the correct method.
    • Rate the operator while timing, not afterwards.
    • Drop abnormal readings (thread breaks, interruptions).
    • Define clear start and end points for each cycle.
    FAQ

    Frequently asked questions

    Are SAM and SMV different?
    No. SAM (Standard Allowed Minutes) and SMV (Standard Minute Value) mean the same thing: the standard time, including allowances, to complete an operation or garment. Different factories and buyers simply use different names.
    How do I choose the right allowance %?
    Most apparel factories use a total of 12–20%. Start with around 10% personal, fatigue & delay, then add machine delay (2–5%) and bundle handling (1–4%) based on the machine and how work is fed. Keep allowances consistent across styles so SAMs stay comparable.
    What is a good line efficiency?
    It depends on product complexity and style changes, but many garment lines run between 50% and 70%. Consistently above 65% is generally considered good; below 50% usually points to balancing, absenteeism, or loss-time issues worth investigating.
    Why are some readings crossed out?
    With “Ignore abnormal readings” switched on, any reading more than 25% away from the median is excluded from the average. These are usually interruptions such as thread breaks or material shortages. Switch the option off to include every reading.
    Can I use this outside the garment industry?
    Yes. The time study formulas are universal — any manual or machine-paced operation can be studied the same way. The machine list and allowance guidance are tuned for apparel, so adjust them to your process.
    Is my data saved?
    The garment operation list is saved only in your own browser so it’s still there when you come back. Nothing is sent to a server.
